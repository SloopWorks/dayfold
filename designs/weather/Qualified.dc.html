One card, two truths

The curator authors one card and attaches a condition. When the device confirms the condition against a fresh forecast for that place, the card carries a weather chip — one quiet caption-row chip naming the match, in the same derived family as “Pickup at 3:00”. When there is no forecast, a stale one, or a provider error, the gate fails open: the card still shows, and the chip is gone — not greyed, not dashed, not “unavailable”.

The whole design rests on one authoring rule: the card’s own copy is condition-neutral. The title and body are true on a dry day. The forecast only ever adds a line of evidence, so removing it leaves an ordinary card behind rather than a hole where a claim used to be.

Every placeholder is a claim

These are the four shapes a designer reaches for when a value is missing. Each one tells the family the app tried to know the weather — which is a fact about the app, not about their day, and which invites a retry the user cannot perform. The correct treatment is the fourth column of the row above: render nothing, and let the card be a card.

In the feed

A confirmed match is what puts the card in NOW — the boost is expressed by position, never by extra ornament. There is no ring, no tint, no urgency colour. The unverified feed below is the same day with the forecast unavailable: the card holds its place and simply says less. Note also what is not in the app bar — the old prototype’s “68°F, rain later” subtitle is retired here. A standing readout is a weather widget, and it is unverifiable copy on every other day.
