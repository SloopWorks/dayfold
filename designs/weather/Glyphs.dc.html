Six conditions the device names itself

The author writes one word — rain. The device reads the forecast for that place and picks the glyph and the wording, so intensity can never contradict live conditions. The set is closed: rain · snow · wind · hot · cold · clear. An unknown condition is ignored, never rendered as a fallback glyph.

Nouns are filled. States of the world are not.

The existing 18 authored icons are nouns — a school, a passport, a car — and they render filled, on an accent tile, because they stand for something the family owns or is planning. The six weather glyphs are conditions, and they render unfilled, at chip scale, in the neutral on-surface-variant role. Same typeface, same weight, same optical size; opposite fill. That single axis is what keeps the additions native to the set instead of a bolt-on — and it also encodes who chose the glyph.

Neither wins, because they never share a slot

The question assumes a collision. There isn’t one, once you notice where media.icon already lives: per ADR 0036 it renders on the kind chip and an optional leading thumbnail — the slots that say what this card is. The weather glyph belongs to the why chip — the slot that says why it is here now. Every card in the derived feed already carries both kinds of caption. So the rule is a slot rule, not a priority rule:

The device never writes into an authored slot, and no author ever writes a weather glyph.
